# Cloud Native Computing Foundation About

CNCF stands for Cloud Native Computing Foundation — a vendor-neutral foundation under the Linux Foundation that hosts and governs open source projects in the cloud native infrastructure space. Kubernetes is the most well-known CNCF project.

CNCF has three maturity levels for hosted projects:

**Sandbox** — early-stage projects that the CNCF considers interesting and worth nurturing. Low bar for entry. No guarantee of production readiness or long-term sustainability. Think of it as "we are watching this."

**Incubating** — projects that have demonstrated growth, a healthy contributor base, adoption by multiple organisations in production, and a governance structure that is not dependent on a single vendor. The project has passed a due diligence process. Think of it as "this is real and being used seriously."

**Graduated** — the highest level. Projects that have reached broad production adoption, a mature governance model, and a demonstrated commitment to long-term sustainability. Kubernetes, Prometheus, Envoy, and Fluentd are examples. Think of it as "this is foundational infrastructure."

**What Keycloak's Incubating status means practically:**

Keycloak entered CNCF Incubating in April 2023. It means the CNCF has formally recognised Keycloak as a serious, production-ready project with multi-vendor adoption and sound governance — not solely dependent on Red Hat. It is a meaningful signal of project health and longevity, distinct from Red Hat's commercial backing. The two together — CNCF governance plus Red Hat commercial support — make Keycloak the lowest-risk long-term bet in the IAM space from a project sustainability perspective.

For the radar entry it is worth noting because it is a governance signal relevant to the Universal Cake dimension of emergence and adaptability — a project under foundation governance is more resilient to key-contributor departure or commercial acquisition than one governed by a single company.
